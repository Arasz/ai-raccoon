## 2. #367's root cause is four competing hypotheses, not one

The issue says the target chunk is *"a dense markdown table row packing three unrelated
definitions"*. **Direct inspection of the chunk contradicts that**, and the inspection outweighs the
issue text.

`entries.id = 18336`, `chunk_index 7/64`, 1012 chars, from
`/Users/arasz/RiderProjects/ai-badger/docs/retrieval.md`. Contents in order: a prose tail from the
previous section (`"on every line."`); the heading `### From a record to a fixture`; ~150 words of
prose about eval fixture bias, issue #140, `tooling/fixture_harvest.py` and
`mcp_queries_observed.jsonl`; then a 4-row table under `| Bucket | What it means |` whose last row is
the match — `| clipped | The stored text hit the 200-char field cap, so it is a prefix, not the
query. |`.

Its sibling `id = 18340`, `chunk_index 9/64`, **opens with the clipped row again** (carried by the
48-token overlay), continues with rows `machine` and `harvestable`, then unrelated prose about
redaction levels. **The header `| Bucket | What it means |` appears only in chunk 7.**

Established:

- **The row is a clean `term | definition` pair.** What packs unrelated meanings is the *chunk*:
  prose + heading + four unrelated table rows. The clipped definition is roughly **one eighth** of
  the embedded text; the other seven eighths are the dilution.
- **The table spans chunks 7 and 9 with the header only in 7** — chunk 9's rows are header-orphaned.
  This is ADR-0048's scope amendment ("33 of 34 chunks carry orphaned body rows with no header";
  0048:47-55) hit live in production data.
- **The overlay duplicates the clipped row into two chunks**, so two near-copies compete in
  retrieval. Effect unknown; measure it.

Four candidate contributors to "FTS ranks it #1, hybrid ranks it #18":

| | Hypothesis | Addressed by |
|---|---|---|
| **H-a** | prose and table share a chunk, so the embedding averages a definition with 150 words about something else | WP4 axis 1 |
| **H-b** | intra-table granularity is wrong — a row's meaning is diluted by its neighbours, and orphaned rows carry no column semantics | WP4 axis 2 |
| **H-c** | `chunk_index` is not document order (#371), so `SourceAffinityRanker`'s adjacency boost fires on non-neighbours and misses real ones | **WP2** |
| **H-d** | RRF with equal weights lets a confident FTS leg be outvoted | WP6 |

**H-c deserves top billing.** ADR-0006 credits *source-affinity ranking* — not the RRF formula — with
fixing the earlier C2 case whose symptom was **hybrid rank 18**, the identical symptom in #367. On
the real bank, 908 of 1,343 source files (68%) were ingested incrementally, and of the re-ingested
files with ≥8 uniquely-locatable chunks, **59 of 59 have `chunk_index` out of document order** (up to
13 inversions in 66 chunks). `retrieval.md` — the file holding the target chunk — measures 10
inversions in 60 chunks.

**The research must attribute, not merely improve.** A chunking change measured on a bank whose
ordering is simultaneously repaired would credit the chunker with a fix that came from the ordering.
That confound is why WP2 ships first.

### Decisions

**Derived expected side.** Expected shape = run the live `Ddl` into a scratch `Data Source=:memory:`
with `EnableExtensions()`/`LoadVector()` and read it back — the technique proven at
`MemorySchemaDigestTests.cs:108-117` and `MemorySchemaVersionTests.cs:919-928`. **No hand-maintained
expected-schema list exists in this package.** The scratch DB needs no encryption key.

**Structural introspection, never SQL text.** `sqlite_master.sql` stores the statement as written —
whitespace, comments, `IF NOT EXISTS`, quoting — and `Ddl` is a raw string literal full of comments
and indentation. A bank created by an older, differently-formatted release would differ in text and
be identical in structure; SQLite normalisation also varies across versions. Text comparison would
make `doctor` a false-positive generator, which for a verb whose whole product is trust is fatal.
Pragma output is already normalised by SQLite. **Triggers have no pragma** — compare existence and
owning table only, and say so in the output rather than implying bodies were checked. Same for
`CHECK` bodies: excluded, and the exclusion is printed.

**Never `OpenBankAsync`** — it opens `ReadWriteCreate` and calls `EnsureAsync`, so inspecting would
migrate. Copy the read-only shape from `AppRegistrations.cs:93-126`:
`SqliteConnectionStringBuilder { Mode = SqliteOpenMode.ReadOnly, Password = (await
IEncryptionKeyResolver.ResolveAsync(ct)).Passphrase }`, `EnableExtensions()`, `LoadVector()`, no
`EnsureAsync`. Sanctioned by ADR-0075:38,88-91.

**Never repairs.** No `--apply`, no flag reserved for one. A wrong-shaped table may hold data the
right shape cannot represent.

**Encrypted banks.** Key resolution / open failure reuse `ExitCode.FailedToResolveEncryptionKey` (1)
and `FailedToOpenEncryptedBank` (2). A bank that cannot be opened has an **unknown** shape, not a
wrong one, and the output must use that word.

**Reports `user_version` and the digest as context lines, not findings.** A digest mismatch alone is
normal (it means `Ddl` runs on the next read-write open) and must not raise the exit code. These two
lines are the difference between "your bank diverged" and "your binary is older than your bank".

**No `--json`.** Nothing in the repo parses CLI output. Deliberate deferral, recorded as such.

**A thin frame.** `IBankCheck` with exactly one implementation, collected as `IEnumerable<IBankCheck>`
and iterated. That buys the derived-list property (A7) for one interface. No second speculative
implementation, no severity ladder, no discovery mechanism.

## WP2 — `chunk_index` is document order (#371). Ships first.

github.com/Arasz/ai-raccoon/issues/371

### The defect

`chunk_index` is assigned by `ROW_NUMBER() ... ORDER BY id` — insertion order. Incremental re-ingest
appends new chunks with the highest ids, so a paragraph inserted into the **middle** of a document
produces a chunk ordered **last**. 68% of source files were ingested incrementally; 59 of 59
measurable files are out of order. `SourceAffinityRanker`'s λ boost (ADR-0005) and its consolidation
both test strict adjacency `|Δchunk_index| == 1`, so on most files they boost non-neighbours and miss
real ones.

### How position is carried

**Recommendation: write position at insert time into the existing `chunk_index` column. No new
column, no ladder step.**

The chunker already emits `IReadOnlyList<string> chunks` in document order. Position is `i`;
`total_chunks` is `chunks.Count`. Both are known at the insert site and both columns already exist
with exactly these semantics. The bug is not missing information — correct information available at
insert is thrown away and then wrongly re-derived from row ids.

Rejected: a `document_ordinal` or `line_span` column on `entries`. It would need a `user_version`
ladder step **and** a 26k-row write, to store a number `chunk_index` is already defined to hold.
What would change this: wanting byte offsets for citation-precision snippets — a different feature.

**Consequence — `RecomputeChunkColumnsForContext` and `RecomputeChunkColumnsBankWide` are deleted.**
Once position is authoritative at insert, a statement re-deriving it from id order is not redundant,
it *is* the bug: it would overwrite correct values with wrong ones. Callers re-pointed:

| Caller | Disposition |
|---|---|
| `FileIngestor.cs:203` | replaced by stamping position from the chunk list (WP3 stamps **all** rows of the source, survivors included) |
| `SqliteMemoryStore.cs:145, 673, 783` (via `:1063-1068`) | same — `memory_write` chunks are also emitted in order (ADR-0064) |
| `ChunkBackfill.cs:96` | pieces of a split row inherit the parent's span; the source is then **marked position-unknown** and the repair job fixes it from the file |
| `SyncService.cs:399` | **the honest case** — merged rows arrive from another bank and their order is not recoverable without the file. Stop *asserting* an order; mark those sources position-unknown. |
| `MemorySchema.cs:1123, :1188` | historical ladder backfills; on a fresh bank they run against no rows. Leave them untouched (editing a stamped ladder step is its own hazard) — the repair job supersedes their output. |

### `source_state` — one new table, serving both #371 and option B

The owner ruled **option B with A**: a version marker for detection plus replacement semantics on
ingest. One object serves both, at the granularity every operation uses:

```sql
CREATE TABLE IF NOT EXISTS source_state (
    ctx             TEXT NOT NULL,
    source_file     TEXT NOT NULL,
    chunker_version INTEGER NOT NULL,
    position_known  INTEGER NOT NULL DEFAULT 0,
    updated_at      INTEGER NOT NULL,
    PRIMARY KEY (ctx, source_file)
);
```

- **`(ctx, source_file)` is exactly the key** `RecomputeChunkColumnsForContext` partitions by and the
  key WP3's reconciliation operates on. A marker at a different granularity from the operation it
  gates is how a marker starts lying — which is why `memory_source` is **not** the home: it is keyed
  `(source_type, source_locator, COALESCE(section,''))`, per *section*, and carries no ctx.
- **`chunker_version`** is option B's marker: a single `ChunkingDefaults.ChunkerVersion` constant,
  bumped by any change to chunk boundaries. A source behind it is stale, so the sweep can answer
  "which rows are stale?" — the question nobody could answer when ADR-0069's 6,240 duplicates
  appeared.
- **`position_known`** is #371's honesty flag, for sources whose ordering was never established from
  the document.
- **A settings fingerprint was rejected** as strictly dominated: all-or-nothing, no per-source
  resumability, and it cannot express `position_known` at all.

**No `user_version` bump** (fact 11). `CurrentVersion` stays **10**; the ADR-0075 digest gate rolls
the new `Ddl` out on the next open. **`MemorySchemaDdlStatementCountTests` pins 39 statements (42 for
a full `EnsureAsync`)** — adding a table moves that count, and updating the pin is part of this
package. The pin exists precisely to make an unnoticed `Ddl` change impossible.

### The repair job for existing banks

**It must not re-ingest.** Re-ingesting under today's insert-only path would *add* rows beside the
stale ones — ADR-0069's accident. The repair is **in place, matched by hash**:

For each `(ctx, source_file)` whose file still exists: read the file, chunk it with the current
chunker, build `hash → position`, then for each stored row of that source
`UPDATE entries SET chunk_index = <position>, total_chunks = <count> WHERE hash = ...`. No inserts,
no deletes, no re-embed. Stamp `position_known = 1` and the current `chunker_version`.

A stored row whose hash appears in no fresh chunk is **stale** — the chunker changed since it was
written. Leave it; leave `position_known = 0` for that source. **This is option B's detection half,
delivered free**: the same pass that repairs ordering enumerates exactly which sources carry stale
rows, which is what WP3's sweep acts on.

**The 1,304 unrepairable rows** — 1,143 whose `source_file` names a gone file, 161 with none at all.
Their position is not recoverable from the database. They keep the `chunk_index` they have and their
source keeps `position_known = 0`. **Silently treating that number as document order is what the
current code does and is the defect.** So `SourceAffinityRanker` must skip the adjacency boost and
the consolidation merge for candidates whose source is not `position_known`. Not boosting is a
correct answer under uncertainty; boosting on a number that means "insertion order" is not.

Registered as an `IMaintenanceJob`, `Interval => null` (once per bank, ever) per ADR-0070, with
`HasWorkAsync` true when any source has `position_known = 0` and a live file — so it re-fires after a
sync merge or a `ChunkBackfill` marks sources unknown.

## WP3 — Ingest is a three-way set reconciliation (#367 foundation)

**Owner ruling: do not lose per-row metadata.** Replacement is a set reconciliation within
`(ctx, source_file)`, not delete-all-then-insert.

Because `hash = ContentHash.Of(path, value) = SHA256(UTF8(path) ‖ UTF8(value))`
(`ContentHash.cs:12-23`), an unchanged chunk re-hashes identically:

| Partition | Action |
|---|---|
| `old ∩ new` | **leave the row entirely untouched** — `rating`, `access_count`, `last_accessed_at`, `created_at`, `embedding`, `structure_embedding` all survive; **no re-embed**. Only `chunk_index`/`total_chunks` are re-stamped from the new position. |
| `old ∖ new` | **DELETE** — the stale chunk today's insert-only path leaves behind forever |
| `new ∖ old` | **INSERT** and embed |

On a typical single-section edit this is a few deletes and inserts against an otherwise untouched
file — **cheaper than today's path**, which re-hashes everything, inserts the changed ones, and
removes nothing.

| `src/AiRaccoon.Infrastructure/Ingestion/FileIngestor.cs` | `InsertChunksAsync` → `ReconcileChunksAsync`: build the fresh hash list, read the stored hash set for bucket+source, apply the three partitions in one transaction, stamp position on every surviving and new row, upsert `source_state`. |
| `src/AiRaccoon.Infrastructure/Sqlite/MemorySql.cs` | `SelectEntryHashesForSourceInBucket`, `DeleteEntriesByHashInBucket`, `StampChunkPosition`, `UpsertSourceState` |

would be fabricating data. This supersedes ADR-0069's acceptance of metadata loss **for the ingest
path**; `ChunkBackfill` itself is out of scope and unchanged.

## WP4 — Research: what is the best chunk shape for a markdown table?

**Not implementation with a report attached, and no arm is privileged.** No production file changes.
Use the `evidence-first-research` skill; every finding carries how it is known (measured / read /
inferred / unverified).

**Record:** `docs/work/2026-08-17-markdown-table-chunk-shape-research.md` (+ self-contained HTML view).
**Baseline: a bank with WP2 merged and the repair job run.** Measuring against unrepaired ordering
would attribute H-c's effect to whatever else changed.

### The question

> **What is the best chunk shape for markdown tables, measured?**
> And, separately: how much of #367's rank comes from each of the four candidate contributors?

### The design is two axes, not six point measurements

Prose/table separation and intra-table granularity are **independent**. A six-arm list would confound
them and could not say how much came from each. So:

**Axis 1 — prose/table separation**
- **1a.** off — a table is N ordinary lines under the greedy packer (status quo)
- **1b.** on — split at table boundaries; prose and tables never share a chunk

**Axis 2 — intra-table granularity**
- **2a.** none — the table's lines pack greedily (status quo)
- **2b.** whole table as one chunk while it fits the token budget
- **2c.** one chunk per row, header row carried in (ADR-0048's acknowledged-unbuilt header carry-over)
- **2d.** one chunk per cell
- **2e.** row linearized into natural language — *"For Bucket `clipped`, what it means is: the stored
  text hit the 200-char field cap, so it is a prefix, not the query."*

**Axis 3 (modifier, applied to the axis-2 arms that produce headingless fragments)**
- **3a.** off
- **3b.** enclosing section heading prepended

Axis 3 is not decoration. `HeadingPathParser.Parse` re-scans each chunk's own text, so a bare row or
cell carries no heading, gets an empty `section`, and **forfeits the 4× bm25 `section` weight**
(`MemorySql.cs:109-119`). Any axis-2 arm finer than 2b is being compared unfairly without it.

**The grid to run** (not the full cross-product — that is 20 cells and most are uninformative):

| Cell | 1 | 2 | 3 | Purpose |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **A** | 1a | 2a | — | baseline / status quo |
| **B** | 1b | 2a | — | axis-1 effect alone — the cheapest possible change |
| **C** | 1b | 2b | — | whole-table |
| **D** | 1b | 2c | 3b | row + header |
| **E** | 1b | 2d | 3b | per cell |
| **F** | 1b | 2e | 3b | linearized row |
| **D′** | 1b | 2c | 3a | isolates axis 3's contribution |
| **G** | 1b | 2a | — with overlay suppressed across a table boundary | the duplicate-row effect |

**A→B is axis 1's effect. B→{C,D,E,F} is axis 2's effect given axis 1. D vs D′ is axis 3's.** The
record must report those differences as named quantities, not a ranked list of eight numbers.

### Arm F deserves explicit treatment

Linearizing a row into a sentence is a serious contender and also the most consequential arm:

- **For it:** pipe syntax is not prose; embedding models handle a sentence far better than
  `| a | b |`. It expresses the owner's "for this column and this row, the value is X" *literally*
  rather than structurally. It composes with 2c (linearize each row) and with 2d.
- **Against it:** the emitted text is no longer a substring of the source. **ADR-0048 already
  crossed that line** (0048:72-77) and set the governing rule: *the payload is preserved exactly, in
  order, and that is what the tests pin*. Arm F must be held to the same rule — every cell value
  present, in column order, nothing invented beyond the connective words — and the record must say
  whether it can be. It also names a consequence ADR-0048 accepted and could not fix: hash churn
  meaning "a re-ingest inserts new rows rather than deduplicating". **WP3 resolves that**, which is
  why arm F is cheaper to adopt now than it would have been in ADR-0048's time. Say so.
- **The unverified risk to check:** an FTS query for the literal string `clipped` still matches, but a
  query containing pipe-table syntax or exact column names may not. Measure the FTS-only rank under
  arm F (falsifier F5 below).

### Falsifiers — what would rule each arm out

State these before measuring; record the arms that lost **with their numbers**, so nobody
re-proposes them. ADR-0072's virtue is that it says precisely what lost and why.

| # | Falsifier | Rules out |
|---|---|---|
| **F1** | Cell A (baseline, post-#371) already reaches the target at rank ≤ 5 | the whole chunking package — H-c dominated |
| **F2** | Cell B reaches rank ≤ 5 | every arm finer than B; row splitting is unpaid-for complexity |
| **F3** | An arm does not beat the best cheaper arm by more than the held-out measurement's own spread (ADR-0058 measured a ±0.03 band on n=3 and refused to choose inside it) | that arm |
| **F4** | An arm's entry-count multiplier on the real-bank copy exceeds **2×** | that arm. 5,799 chunks (22.3%) contain a table line, 4,396 (16.9%) two or more, 2,370 (9.1%) a header separator. Embed time, `vec_entries` size, KNN candidate density and file size all scale with row count. |
| **F5** | An arm moves the target **off FTS rank 1** | that arm — it broke the leg that was already right in order to fix the leg that was wrong |
| **F6** | An arm's emitted text loses or reorders a cell value (arm F specifically) | arm F, under ADR-0048's own rule |
| **F7** | An arm breaks any existing chunking guarantee — fence balance or content budget (`ChunkingCorpusGuaranteeTests`) | that arm |

**Report the measured value of F4 and F5 for every arm, not only the winner.**

### Cost is part of the verdict, not a footnote

Each arm implies a different re-chunk blast radius over the **24,689 re-derivable rows**. The record
must report per arm: entry-count multiplier (F4), estimated re-embed time, resulting bank size, and —
for arm F — the fact that emitted text is generated. WP3 makes the migration itself cheap (one
`ChunkerVersion` bump arms the sweep), so cost here is about the *steady-state* bank, not the
migration. An arm that wins on retrieval by a margin inside F3's band while tripling the bank has not
won.

### The ADR-0056 / ADR-0072 constraint, carried honestly

The offline corpus is 44 queries, 19 gradeable, 3 held-out (A8/A9/A10, floors `0.131205`, `0.553146`,
`0.169580`). In-sample mean nDCG@5 is 0.673 against held-out 0.285 — **out-of-sample retrieval is 42%
of the published figure** (ADR-0056). ADR-0058 refused a better-scoring configuration because
"selecting it because it scored 0.3333 would be tuning on the held-out set"; ADR-0072 refused because
choosing from a 3-query column is "fitting noise on n=3", and added that the gate corpus "cannot even
see the defect class" and that "a corpus built alongside the change it validates is precisely the
trap this project has been caught by before".

Applied here:

1. **The #367 query is a single named counterexample**, reported as one, never as a metric.
2. **The 44-query corpus supplies the non-regression side only.** It can prove an arm broke nothing
   (F7, and `ParityGateTests`' `NdcgParityDelta = 0.02`). It cannot elect a winner.
3. **The real bank (25,993 entries) is an observation and inspection surface, read-only — not a gate
   and not something to tune on.** Copy it; never open the original read-write. Findings from it are
   evidence about *what the data looks like*, not scores.
4. **Do not manufacture a table-query corpus alongside this change.** That is the named trap.
5. **"No arm can be adjudicated on the available evidence" is a permitted and possibly correct
   output.** If it is the outcome, the record must say **what evidence would settle it** — most
   plausibly held-out capacity: queries whose expected documents no sweep has touched, in enough
   number that ±0.03 is signal (ADR-0058's own "what would unblock it"). WP7 is conditional; the plan
   is not embarrassed if WP4 kills it.

### Gate for WP4

The `research-record-audit` skill over the finished record. It must show: every finding's evidence
class; every number's producing command; F1-F7 answered per arm; the A→B, B→{C,D,E,F} and D vs D′
differences reported as named quantities; losing arms recorded with numbers; and cost per arm. A
record that names a winner without a measured F4 multiplier and a measured F5 FTS rank for that arm
is incomplete and fails the audit.

## WP5 — Research: fusion confidence heuristic

**Record:** `docs/work/2026-08-17-fusion-confidence-heuristic-research.md`. Baseline also post-WP2.

### The question

> Both per-leg ranks and per-leg raw scores are already in memory at the fusion point
> (`SqliteMemoryStore.cs:259`). Is there a rule keyed on cross-leg disagreement that rescues #367's
> shape without demoting the many queries where the vector leg is the one that is right?

### Candidate rules — measure all three, lead with the third

- *Rank-1 rescue*: a floor on the fused score of anything at rank 1 in either leg.
- *Confidence-weighted RRF*: per-leg weight scaled by that leg's own score separation between rank 1
  and rank k — a leg with a flat score curve is a leg with no opinion.
- **`max(rrf, best_single_leg_normalized)`** — direct enforcement of ADR-0006:49-50's declared rule
  that *"the hybrid never ranks the expected chunk below the best single modality"*.

**Lead with the third.** #367 is a counterexample to a rule the project already claims to hold. The
cleanest framing is not "add a heuristic" but "enforce the invariant ADR-0006 declared and never
enforced" — a smaller change, easier to justify, and its asymmetry is its safety property: it can
only *raise* a result some leg was confident about, never lower one, so it cannot demote a vector-leg
winner (which gets the same floor).

### What the record must establish

**Q1 — absent ≠ low-ranked.** `Fuse` iterates only lists containing the hash
(`ReciprocalRankFusion.cs:26-39`, pinned by `Fuse_EmptyModalityList_DoesNotEmptyTheResult`), so a
missing leg contributes **0**, not a sentinel worst rank. Measure how often each occurs on the real
bank; the rule must treat them differently.

**Q2 — degradation, enumerated and measured.** Skip the rule when: `queryVector is null`
(`EmbedQueryAsync` returns null with no engine; `SearchAsync:219` then skips the vector leg);
`VectorWeight == 0` or `FtsWeight == 0` (the reproduction path itself); the vector batch is empty
because rows are pending-embedding with no `vec_entries` row; or the FTS leg degraded —
`QueryFtsBatchAsync:826-849` catches `SqliteException` and falls back silently, **currently
unobservable at the fusion point**. Also note `StructureFusion` scores absent structure similarity as
0 (ADR-0057). Report how often each fires; a rule reading "leg unavailable" as "leg disagreed" would
fire on every search in an unembedded bank.

**Q3 — the harm side, which is the whole question.** Per rule, on the 44-query corpus and the
held-out tier: how many queries change order at all, and of those, how many get worse. **Quantify
the harm; do not assert its absence.**

### Falsifiers

- **G1.** No rule moves the target above rank 5 → does not ship in any form.
- **G2.** The winning rule changes order on a meaningful share of the 44 gate queries while the
  held-out mean moves less than ADR-0058's ±0.03 band → *active but unadjudicable*, the ADR-0072
  condition. Does not kill the package; it is the recorded reason the flag defaults off.
- **G3.** The winning rule degrades `ParityGateTests` nDCG@10 by more than `NdcgParityDelta = 0.02`
  at any sweep point when enabled → does not ship even off.

**Expected outcome, stated in advance so it cannot be claimed as a finding later:** G2 is likely.
ADR-0058 and ADR-0072 both landed there on smaller changes.

## 5. What I would not do, and what looks over-engineered

**Would not build:**

1. **`--json` on `doctor`.** No consumer; a JSON shape is a compatibility surface. Deferred, recorded.
2. **A repair path on `doctor`.** The issue forbids it and the reason is good.
3. **A `document_ordinal` or `line_span` column on `entries`.** `chunk_index` already means this. A
   new column costs a ladder step and a 26k-row write to store a number the schema already has a
   place for.
4. **A `user_version` ladder step 11 for `source_state`.** A new table belongs in the unconditional
   `Ddl` (`MemorySchema.cs:44-47`). The ladder-coordination problem does not arise.
5. **A `SchemaShape` DSL, a severity ladder, or a second `IBankCheck`.** One interface, one
   implementation, one derived list.
6. **A new table for the fusion diff metrics.** `metrics` takes new `name` values by design.
7. **A separate "shadow mode" flag for fusion.** The enabled path already records the baseline order
   alongside the new one, so enabled and observed are the same state. Shadow-only would be a third
   *value* of one setting, not a second setting.
8. **Re-pinning `HeldOutRetrievalGateTests` floors in PR4.** Default-off means those numbers must not
   move; if they do, that is the bug.
9. **Per-source resumability beyond `source_state`'s own row.** Reconciliation is idempotent and a
   failed maintenance job is not stamped (ADR-0070), so a retry is already safe.
10. **The full 20-cell cross-product in WP4.** Eight cells answer the two-axis question; the rest are
    uninformative combinations that would cost real re-embed time.

**On the chunking arms — a prior, not a recommendation.** The chunk shape is WP4's to determine and I
am not pre-empting it. What I can say from the inspection, as a *prior to be tested rather than a
conclusion*: the failing chunk's dilution is ~7/8 unrelated prose, which points at **axis 1** carrying
more of the effect than axis 2 — that is F2's whole purpose, and if F2 fires, the cheapest arm wins
and the rest is unpaid-for complexity. Two specific cautions the arms must be measured against rather
than argued about:

- **Any arm finer than whole-table forfeits the 4× bm25 `section` weight** unless axis 3 is applied,
  because `HeadingPathParser` re-scans each chunk's own text. That is why axis 3 exists and why D vs
  D′ is in the grid. An arm compared without it has been handicapped, not tested.
- **FTS already ranks the target #1.** Any arm that moves it off rank 1 has broken the leg that was
  right to fix the leg that was wrong. That is F5, and it is the falsifier I would watch most closely
  for the finest-grained arms (E and F).

The per-cell arm's specific risk is that it separates `clipped` from its definition — the only
coherent unit the document has — in exactly the case the issue exists for. That is a hypothesis with
a measurement attached (E vs D, and F5), not a reason to drop the arm.

**Can the confidence heuristic help #367 without harming queries where the vector leg is right?**

**Yes in one specific form, no in the general form.** "Boost anything ranked 1 by either leg" is
trivially constructible and would demote every query where the semantic leg carries the meaning — most
of them on this corpus. The form that can work is the **`max(rrf, best_single_leg)` floor**, because
it is *asymmetric*: it can only raise a result some leg was confident about, never lower one, and the
vector-leg winner gets the same floor. It needs no new justification — it is the literal enforcement
of ADR-0006:49-50, a rule the project claims and #367 proves it does not hold. WP5 should test it
first. Residual harm is real — raising everything some leg liked compresses the top of the list and
can displace a genuinely fused result — and that is what G2's harm count quantifies.

**One thing I would push back on if asked:** #367 may not need a chunking change at all. Three of its
four candidate causes (#371, header orphaning, prose/table mixing) are defects with unambiguous fixes
and no adjudication problem; only the fourth is a tuning question. The WP2 decision point and WP4's
F1/F2 exist to find out before WP7 is built, and both should be honoured rather than treated as
formalities.

## 6. Risks that could sink this

**R1 — Ordering repair is credited to the chunker.** The largest methodological risk. Fixing #371 and
changing chunking in overlapping measurements would attribute H-c's improvement to H-a/H-b and produce
a result nobody can reproduce. Mitigation: WP2 ships and merges first, the decision-point experiment
runs on the repaired bank before WP4 begins, and WP4's baseline is the repaired bank.

**R2 — Axis confounding inside WP4.** Reporting one combined number for "table awareness" would hide
whether the benefit came from prose separation or row granularity, and would make the expensive half
unjustifiable and the cheap half invisible. Mitigation: the two-axis grid, with A→B and B→{C,D,E,F}
reported as named quantities, and D vs D′ isolating axis 3.

**R3 — Re-chunking creates duplicate rows in real banks.** ADR-0069's accident, now against 26k rows
with 22% blast radius. Mitigation: WP3 ships separately; D5/B1 are stated as *equalities* on
untouched-source row counts, not as absence of complaints; WP2's repair is explicitly in-place (P3).

**R4 — The sweep destroys the 5% that cannot be regenerated.** 1,143 orphaned + 161 sourceless rows.
"Delete and re-ingest" is ruled out entirely. B5/B6, P5/P6 and D5 are the gates, each watched red
under a predicate-dropping perturbation.

**R5 — Metadata is lost despite the owner's ruling.** A delete-all-then-insert implementation passes
"no stale rows" perfectly and destroys every rating in the bank. **B2 and B3 are the gates and their
red run against a wholesale-replacement implementation must be recorded in PR3.**

**R6 — The flag leaks into the default path.** Symptom: any existing fusion test needs editing.
`ReciprocalRankFusionTests` pins `61.0/62`; `SearchResultMergerTests` pins the closed form at 1e-12.
**Treat a required edit to those files as a defect in WP6, not a test to update.** C1's
red-perturbation runs deliberately once, output in the PR.

**R7 — The heuristic misreads a degraded leg as a disagreeing leg.** With no engine configured
`queryVector` is null; mid-ingest, rows are pending and absent from `vec_entries`; and
`QueryFtsBatchAsync` swallows `SqliteException` invisibly. A rule reading any of these as disagreement
would fire on every search in exactly the banks least able to tolerate it. WP6a exists solely to make
the FTS degradation observable; C6/C7/C8 are separate gates because the causes fail independently.

**R8 — The ADR-0072 trap: a corpus built alongside the change it validates.**
- *#371*: not exposed — a bug fix with a deterministic gate (P1 fails today by construction).
- *Fusion*: mitigated structurally — default-off, evidence from live `search_quality` outcomes on
  queries nobody selected; sample size named in ADR-0077 before collection.
- *Chunking*: **not fully mitigable, and the plan's largest exposure.** Re-pinning held-out floors
  after the change that moved them is fitting; a table-query corpus assembled now by the person making
  the change is the named trap. The reversal probe is the only unfittable check, which is why WP7 step
  4 makes it a hard stop, and WP4 predicting direction before measuring is the only other defence. If
  neither holds, the correct outcome is ADR-0072's: measure, write the ADR, ship nothing.

**R9 — Arm F emits text that loses a cell value.** Linearization is generation, and generation can
drop things. ADR-0048's rule — preserve the payload exactly, in order, and pin that — is the
constraint; D3 and F6 are the gates. Without them, arm F could win on a metric while silently
truncating the bank's tabular content.

**R10 — `position_known` is added and then ignored.** Adding an honesty flag `SourceAffinityRanker`
does not consult would be worse than not adding it — it would look like the problem was handled. P7 is
the gate; watch it red against a ranker that boosts regardless.

**R11 — WP4 returns "no arm is adjudicable" and the chunking half does not close.** Likely enough to
plan for, and not a failure mode: ADR-0058 and ADR-0072 both ended there and were right. #367 would
close on #371 + WP3 + WP6 plus a documented reason and a statement of what evidence would settle it;
PR2/PR3 ship regardless.

**R12 — `Ddl` statement-count pin.** Adding `source_state` moves the count
`MemorySchemaDdlStatementCountTests` pins (ADR-0075 measured 39/42). Updating it is expected and part
of WP2; the pin exists to make an unnoticed `Ddl` change impossible and should be seen to fire.

**R13 — main moves under a long-running worktree.** Documented repo hazard: merge main in, never
rebase a shared branch, push after every commit, merge main before opening a PR from a long-lived
branch.
